# The Hidden Barriers to AI in Hospitals Aren't Just Technical

When people ask why AI isn't everywhere in healthcare, they usually point to hardware and bandwidth. Those matter. But the blockers I see most often are human: how clinicians are trained, how workflows are shaped, and how trust is earned.

A model with strong metrics on paper can still fail quietly if it adds friction during a busy clinic or if it makes decisions opaquely. For low-resource hospitals, "extra steps" are not neutral — they reduce patient throughput. What works is incremental, understandable assistance that plugs into current routines: triage nudges, differential reminders, better study support for students. The goal isn't to replace clinicians; it's to give them reliable scaffolding. That starts with education and honest validation, not just benchmarks.
